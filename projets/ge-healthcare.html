GE HealthCare

A LinkedIn awareness campaign for an interventional X-ray imaging system, ahead of the launch of a new feature. The goal was to make cardiologists recognize the clinical problem the feature solves: getting clear images for patients with a high BMI.

01 — Project overview

Complex technology, made clear.

ClientGE HealthCare
Format13-post LinkedIn series with clinical image videos
AudienceInterventional cardiologists and cath lab teams
RoleMarketing & communication, copywriting
LanguageEnglish
02 — Context

Set up the story before the launch.

For patients with a high BMI, X-rays must pass through thicker layers of soft tissue, which often makes images less clear. This first phase of the campaign built awareness of that problem, so the feature announcement would land with an audience that already recognized it.

03 — Approach

One pain point, many angles.

  1. One pain point, many angles

    The campaign ran as a series of 13 posts. Each one explored a different side of the challenge: image quality, radiation dose, motion artifacts, positioning, and procedure time.

  2. Question-led hooks

    Every post opens with a question cardiologists face in practice.

  3. Clear structure

    Clinical problem, then its impact on the doctor’s work, then how the system responds.

  4. Accurate and compliant

    Technical concepts in plain language, with every claim kept within the regulations that apply to medical communication.

  5. Text and image together

    Each post was paired with a short video of real clinical images, matched to the copy so readers could see the result the text described.

04 — Selected posts

Clinical questions, clear answers.

The campaign included 13 posts. Here are four of them, published in English on LinkedIn.

Post 01 / 13

How to avoid the obstruction of key angles for large patients in interventional X-ray imaging

In case of excess fat, especially in the chest or abdomen, key angles may be hindered in X-ray imaging. This affects the X-ray tube rotation, makes the coronary arteries less clear, and often means repositioning or extra projections, which lengthens the procedure. The system’s small footprint helps here: it reaches steeper angulations with the 30-cm detector configuration.

Post 02 / 13

Why is contrast resolution reduced for large patients in coronary angioplasty?

Patients with a high BMI often have more adipose tissue between the coronary arteries and the imaging equipment. This can reduce contrast resolution in angiographic images, making it harder to tell the vessels apart from the surrounding tissue. A wide range of focal spots, with enough tube power, helps keep that loss to a minimum.

Post 03 / 13

How to avoid suboptimal contrast filling of vessels in large patients

Even with optimal contrast injection, the extra soft tissue in patients with a high BMI can cause suboptimal filling in the vessels. With high peak power and smaller focal spots, the system increases spatial resolution by 22%, for clearer, more detailed images.

Post 04 / 13

Why does obesity decrease image quality in percutaneous coronary interventions?

Obesity increases the thickness of soft tissue, which makes blood vessels, wires, and stents harder to see. The goal: help cardiologists see coronary arteries and devices clearly, for every patient.
